Why do we need this interactive system?

When offline products are scattered, packaging visuals look the same, and consumers lack information about the origin, a handmade display board you can take with you handles the 'seeing,' while a QR code takes care of 'digging deeper'.

01

Offline: First, have people stop

The display boards can use hand-drawing, cardboard, stickers, and original illustration styles, making the information feel less like a traditional sign and more like a 'handwritten map' from Chiang Mai. They're great for a quick look on-site and also friendly for visitors who aren't used to interacting with phones.

02

Online: Create a connection with people again

After scanning the QR code, the webpage becomes a second layer of information. Young consumers can click on product cards and see the origin, ingredients, story, and usage through flipping animations. This way, the 'product packaging' no longer has to carry all the information, and the QR code turns static materials into a digital gateway that can be continuously updated.
